# Valuation framework

## The principle
A sophisticated spreadsheet or Python model does not make a valuation reliable. Reliability depends on the basis of value, evidence, assumptions, model selection, validation and professional judgement.

## Explicit DCF workflow
1. Define the valuation purpose, valuation date and basis of value.
2. Understand the property, tenure and occupational interests.
3. Verify passing income and lease events.
4. Establish ERV from appropriate market evidence.
5. Select an explicit forecast period appropriate to the asset and available evidence.
6. Model rent reviews, expiries/breaks, voids, incentives, irrecoverable expenditure, capex and other relevant cash-flow items.
7. Support rental-growth assumptions with market evidence; avoid double counting growth in both cash flow and discount/capitalisation assumptions.
8. Derive a market-consistent discount rate and explain its relationship to risk and observed pricing.
9. Estimate terminal value using an evidenced exit assumption and avoid treating the terminal value as a plug.
10. Discount annual cash flows and terminal value to the valuation date.
11. Run sensitivities and scenarios.
12. Cross-check against transactions and an appropriate implicit model.
13. Reconcile the evidence and record the valuer's conclusion and reasoning.

## Market Value vs Investment Value
A DCF is a model, not a basis of value. For a Market Value exercise, inputs should be consistent with market-participant expectations and observable market evidence where available. Entity-specific inputs can instead lead to Investment Value/worth. The repository keeps that distinction explicit.

## Model risks
- false precision;
- unsupported rental growth;
- unsupported discount or exit rates;
- treating lease breaks as certain events;
- ignoring incentives, voids or capital expenditure;
- double counting risk or growth;
- applying a single ERV indiscriminately across heterogeneous units;
- hindsight bias when comparing a model with a known sale price;
- data transcription and unit errors.

## Data controls
Every real-world case should record source, access date, unit of measurement, whether the datum is asking/transactional evidence, and whether it has been independently verified. Missing evidence is labelled missing rather than invented.

## RICS context
This project is designed around the distinction between growth-implicit income capitalisation and growth-explicit DCF, and around RICS's emphasis on professional judgement in selecting methods and models. It is an educational project, not an RICS-compliant valuation report.
