# Case 02 — Grays industrial open storage: reversionary income

## Why this case matters

This case deliberately differs from the multi-let Glade example. It tests a **single-let, materially under-rented/reversionary investment** where the valuer must distinguish passing income from ERV and think carefully about lease events, tenant break risk and the source of DCF inputs.

## Publicly reported evidence

Source: Allsop investment marketing page for **Land East of Curzon Drive, Manor Way Industrial Estate, Grays, Essex RM17 6BG**.

The public marketing information reports:

- freehold industrial open-storage site;
- approximately 1.56 acres;
- single letting from August 2024 to August 2029;
- tenant-only break in August 2028;
- lease contracted outside the Landlord and Tenant Act 1954 security-of-tenure provisions;
- passing rent: **£170,000 pa / £2.50 psf**;
- stated ERV: **£271,816 pa / £4.00 psf**;
- marketing price: offers in excess of **£2.66m**;
- quoted NIY: **6.01%**;
- marketing also stated prime IOS rents above £5.00 psf.

Public source: https://www.allsop.co.uk/investment-overview/highly-reversionary-industrial-open-storage-investment-let-to-thames-energy-recovery-facility-limited-in-grays/ci00278

## Valuation issue

The passing rent is roughly 62.5% of the stated ERV. A simple capitalisation of current income therefore risks obscuring the reversionary profile. Conversely, adopting ERV immediately would ignore the contractual income stream and timing/risk of achieving market rent.

This makes the asset useful for comparing:

1. a traditional growth-implicit term-and-reversion approach; and
2. a growth-explicit DCF where the contractual rent, break/expiry, re-letting assumptions and future ERV are modelled separately.

## APC judgement points

### Why not just capitalise £170,000?
Because the property is reported as materially under-rented. The contractual income is relevant, but the reversion to market rent is also part of the asset's economics.

### Why not simply use the quoted NIY as the discount rate?
A net initial yield is a market pricing ratio based on current income. A growth-explicit DCF discount rate is a required return applied to explicit future cash flows. They are conceptually different and should not be substituted mechanically.

### What does the break option do?
It creates timing and income risk. The model should test at least a break-exercised and break-not-exercised scenario rather than burying the risk inside a single unexplained number.

### Market Value versus Investment Value
For Market Value, the DCF assumptions should represent market-participant expectations and be grounded in market evidence. Investor-specific hurdle rates or bespoke financing assumptions would instead point toward Investment Value/worth.

## Professional conclusion

This is intentionally **not presented as a Red Book valuation**. The public particulars provide enough evidence to demonstrate modelling, but not enough independently verified evidence to support every input required for a formal Market Value opinion.

The important skill demonstrated is knowing **what is known, what is assumed, what needs verification, and how those matters affect the valuation conclusion**.
